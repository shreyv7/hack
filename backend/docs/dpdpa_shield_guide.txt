1. What is DPDPA?
DPDPA = Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023
It is an Indian law (passed in August 2023) that tells companies:
"If you collect personal data from Indian citizens — like their name, email, phone, health info,
Aadhaar number — you MUST handle it responsibly, or face penalties up to ₹250 Crore."
Think of it like traffic rules, but for data. Just like you can't drive recklessly, companies can't misuse
people's personal data.

Who does it apply to?
Any company (Indian or foreign) that collects/processes personal data of people in India
E-commerce sites, hospitals, banks, insurance companies, SaaS platforms, retail chains, etc.

What happens if a company breaks the rules?
Violation

Penalty

Not following consent rules

Up to ₹50 Crore

Data breach not reported

Up to ₹200 Crore

Children's data mishandled

Up to ₹200 Crore

General non-compliance

Up to ₹250 Crore

2. What is DPDPA Shield?
DPDPA Shield is a SaaS product (Software-as-a-Service) that helps companies become compliant
with the DPDPA law without hiring an army of lawyers and compliance experts.

4. How Does a Company Use DPDPA Shield? (The
Complete Flow)
Step 1: Company Signs Up → Onboarding
Company signs up → Creates workspace → Configures their industry
The company (let's say "ZeroShield Pvt Ltd" — a pharmacy chain):
1. Signs up on DPDPA Shield
2. Creates a Workspace (like an account for their company)
3. Fills in company details in Workspace Info
4. Configures their Industry (Healthcare/Pharmacy) in Industry Config — this loads rules specific to
healthcare
5. Maps their team roles in DPDP Role Mapping (who is the DPO, who is CISO, etc.)
6. Sets up Deployment Settings (how the consent banners will be deployed to their website)
This is the Settings section in the sidebar.

Step 2: Set Up Consent Management
This is the core of DPDPA compliance. The law says:
"Before collecting anyone's data, you MUST ask for their permission (consent), tell them WHY you
need it, and let them withdraw anytime."
Here's how each module works:

🔹 Banner Builder

What: A visual tool to design the cookie/consent popup banner that appears on their website.
Real example: When you visit any website and see "We use cookies. Accept / Reject" — that popup is a
consent banner.
How it works in DPDPA Shield:
The DPO opens Banner Builder
Designs the banner (colors, text, buttons)
Configures what options to show (Accept All, Reject All, Customize)
Deploys it to their website via a JavaScript snippet

🔹 Consent Notices Manager
What: Create and manage the legal notices that tell users exactly what data you're collecting and why.
Real example: The detailed privacy page that says "We collect your name for order delivery, your email for
marketing, your health records for prescriptions..."
How it works:
DPO creates a notice (e.g., "Customer Purchase Consent v2.1")
Attaches purposes to it (why we collect data)
Publishes it → it goes LIVE on the website
Tracks versions (v1.0, v2.0, etc.)
Shows Rule 3 Warnings (compliance issues the system detected)

🔹 Purpose Registry
What: A catalog of all the reasons (purposes) why the company collects data.
Real example:

🔹 Cookie & Tracker Manager
What: Scans the company's website to find ALL cookies and trackers (Google Analytics, Facebook Pixel,
etc.) and categorizes them.
Real example: Your website might be dropping 47 cookies without you even knowing. This tool finds

How it works:
Scans the website URL

DPO categorizes each one
Links cookies to consent purposes
Blocks cookies until user gives consent

🔹 Multi-Language Editor
What: Translates consent notices into India's 22 official languages.
Why needed: DPDPA says notices must be available in the user's preferred language.

🔹 Consent Withdrawal Flow
What: Lets users take back their consent at any time.
Real example: If someone previously said "Yes, send me marketing emails" and now wants to stop — they
can withdraw consent here, and the system stops processing immediately.

🔹 Notice Delivery Channels
What: Configures HOW consent notices reach users — website popup, email, SMS, in-app notification,
WhatsApp, etc.

🔹 Consent Manager Integration
What: Connects DPDPA Shield with your existing tech (CRM, email tools, marketing platforms) via APIs
so that when a user withdraws consent, ALL systems stop processing.

🔹 Proof Vault
What: Stores tamper-proof evidence of every consent — who consented, when, to what, via which
channel.

Why needed: If the government asks "Prove that this user gave consent," the company needs hard
evidence. Proof Vault is that evidence locker.

Step 3: Data Protection
The law says:
"You must know exactly what personal data you have, where it is, and protect it."

🔹 Data Inventory
What: A complete map of ALL personal data in the company — which databases, which columns, which
servers.
Real example: Like doing an inventory of your warehouse, but for data.
Database A → has customer names, emails (2.4M records)
Database B → has health records (120K records)
S3 Bucket → has uploaded ID documents

🔹 PII Scanner
What: Automatically scans databases, files, and APIs to find personal data that might be hiding in
unexpected places.
Real example: You might not know that some developer accidentally stored Aadhaar numbers in a log
file. PII Scanner finds it.
How it works:
Connects to databases, S3 buckets, APIs
Scans all columns/fields
Flags PII (name, email, phone, Aadhaar, PAN, health data)
Shows a dashboard: "342 PII instances found, 45 require masking"

🔹 Data Masking
What: Hides sensitive data so that people who don't need to see it can't.
Real example: A support agent sees XXXX-XXXX-1234 instead of full Aadhaar number.

🔹 Data Anonymisation
What: Permanently removes the ability to identify a person from a dataset.

Real example: For analytics, instead of "Rahul Sharma bought medicine X", it becomes "User_7842
bought product_category_Y".

🔹 Data Sources
What: Manage all the connections to databases, cloud storage, APIs that contain personal data.

🔹 Retention Policies
What: Set rules for how long data is kept and auto-delete it after.
Real example: "Delete customer purchase history after 5 years" or "Remove health records 2 years after
last prescription"
Why needed: DPDPA says you can't keep data forever — only as long as the purpose requires.

🔹 Children's Data
What: Special protections for data of people under 18.
Why needed: DPDPA has EXTRA strict rules for children's data. You need parental consent, can't use it
for targeted advertising, etc. Penalty: up to ₹200 Crore.
How it works:
Identifies records belonging to minors
Ensures parental consent exists
Blocks any marketing/profiling on children's data
Tracks age verification

Step 4: Data Principal Rights (People's Rights)
The law gives every Indian citizen these rights over their data:

🔹 Rights Portal (Public)
What: A public-facing webpage that the company embeds on their website where citizens can exercise
their rights.
The 6 Rights:
1. Access Your Data — "Show me what data you have about me" (DPDPA Section 11)
2. Correct Your Data — "My address is wrong, fix it" (DPDPA Section 12)
3. Erase Your Data — "Delete everything about me" (Right to be Forgotten, DPDPA Section 12)
4. Nominate a Person — "If I die, let my wife handle my data rights" (DPDPA Section 15)

5. File a Grievance — "I have a complaint about how you're using my data"
6. Withdraw Consent — "I no longer want you to use my data for marketing" (DPDPA Section 6)
How it works for the citizen:
1. Citizen visits ZeroShield's website → clicks "Privacy Portal"
2. Sees the 6 rights as cards
3. Clicks "Access Your Data"
4. Fills form (name, email, phone, details)
5. Gets a Request ID (like DSAR-847291 )
6. Receives acknowledgment within 24 hours
7. Gets their data within 30 days
How it works for the company (DPO):

🔹 DSAR SLA Tracker
What: Tracks all incoming rights requests and ensures they are fulfilled within the legal deadline.
SLA = Service Level Agreement — here it means the 30-day legal deadline to respond.
How it works:
Shows all incoming DSARs in a table
Color-coded:

🟢 On track | 🟡 Due soon | 🔴 Overdue

Sends alerts when a deadline is approaching

Tracks response time (e.g., "Average: 18.4 days")

Step 5: Security & Compliance

🔹 Security Safeguards Hub
What: Tracks all the security measures the company has in place — encryption, access controls, audit
trails, etc.
Why needed: DPDPA says you must implement "reasonable security safeguards" to protect personal
data.

🔹 Breach Disclosure Wizard
What: A step-by-step wizard to handle data breaches within the 72-hour legal deadline.
How it works (the 5 steps):
1. Impact Assessment — How bad is it? How many people affected? What data leaked?

2. DPB Notification — Report the breach to the Data Protection Board of India
3. DP Notification — Notify all affected individuals (via email, SMS)
4. Public Disclosure — If required, publish a public statement
5. Submit & Track — Package everything and submit, then track follow-ups
Real example: "287 customer health records got exposed because an S3 bucket was misconfigured. We
have 72 hours to report this to the government and notify all 287 people."
The wizard has a countdown timer (71h 42m remaining) to create urgency.

🔹 Audit Log
What: Records EVERY action taken in the system — who did what, when, from where.
Why needed: For government audits. If regulators ask "Who modified this consent notice and when?" —
the audit log has the answer.

How it works:

Fill in project details
